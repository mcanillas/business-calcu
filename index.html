    Quick guide

    • VAT, income tax and percentage tax are three different taxes. VAT and percentage tax are based on sales. Income tax is based on profit, except under the 8% option.
    • VAT (12%): required once your VATable sales go over ₱3,000,000 a year. You collect output VAT from customers and deduct creditable input VAT paid to VAT-registered suppliers.
    • Percentage tax (3%): for non-VAT businesses selling regular goods or services. It is not due if you are on the 8% option or if your goods are VAT-exempt by law.
    • 8% option: sole proprietors only, non-VAT, gross sales ≤ ₱3M. 8% of gross sales over ₱250,000 replaces both the graduated income tax and the 3% percentage tax.
    • Corporations: 20% income tax if net taxable income ≤ ₱5M and total assets ≤ ₱100M, otherwise 25%. From the 4th year, the 2% Minimum Corporate Income Tax (MCIT) applies if it's higher.

    Payroll notes

    • 13th month pay (1/12 of the year's basic salary) is mandatory and due on or before December 24. It is tax-free up to ₱90,000 together with other bonuses.
    • Withholding tax uses the BIR monthly table in effect from 2023. Minimum wage earners are exempt.
    • Under the law, employers must register their employees with SSS, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG. Unticking the box only removes these from the computation.
    • For semi-monthly payroll, divide the amounts by 2. Check that salaries meet your region's minimum wage (set by the RTWPB).

    How 12% VAT is computed

    Price is before VAT (VAT-exclusive)

    VAT = Price × 12%
    Total = Price + VAT

    Price already includes VAT (VAT-inclusive)

    Sales before VAT = Total ÷ 1.12
    VAT = Total − Sales before VAT

    Taxes & contributions to remit

    VAT, withholding tax, and the employee share of SSS, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG are collected from customers or employees and passed on to the government. They are not part of your expenses. The employer share and the income and percentage taxes are your own costs.
